Interpretation

Timeline of facial and palatal development, mapping which facial prominence contributes what, and when fusion completes.

Prominences by week
  • Week 4: the five facial prominences appear — 1 frontonasal, paired (2) maxillary, paired (2) mandibular prominences.
  • Week 5: nasal (olfactory) placodes invaginate to form nasal pits, splitting the frontonasal prominence into medial and lateral nasal prominences.
  • Weeks 6–7: maxillary prominences grow medially and fuse with the medial nasal prominences to form the upper lip. Merged medial nasal prominences form the philtrum, columella, and primary palate (intermaxillary segment).
Palate
  • Primary palate = intermaxillary segment (from fused medial nasal prominences); becomes the pre-maxilla anterior to the incisive foramen.
  • Secondary palate = fusion of the palatal shelves of the maxillary prominences, closing anterior→posterior and ending at the uvula (~week 12).
  • Lip fusion ~week 10; palate fusion completes ~week 12.
Verified: Any Le Fort fracture by definition involves the pterygoid plates (top-line list here is a fracture aside). Facial-prominence timing above matches standard embryology (Langman/Moore).
Pierre Robin sequence

Triad: micrognathia → glossoptosis (tongue falls back — the note's "macroglossia" is a slip; the tongue is normal-sized but posteriorly displaced) → wide U-shaped cleft palate and airway obstruction. The retropositioned tongue mechanically blocks palatal shelf fusion.

Interpretation

Classification and the operative timeline for cleft repair.

Classification

The Kernahan "striped-Y" is the classic pictorial scheme: the Y's arms map the lip/alveolus/primary palate and the stem maps the secondary (hard + soft) palate, scored by severity. The incisive foramen is the landmark dividing primary palate (anterior) from secondary palate (posterior). Clefts close/present anterior → posterior; the left side is the most common unilateral laterality.

Surgical timeline (augmented, "rule of 10s" context)
Procedure Timing Why then
Cheiloplasty (lip repair) ~3 months (Rule of 10: ~10 wks, 10 lb, Hgb 10) Earlier repair risks maxillary retrusion / reduced length
Palatoplasty ~12–18 months Before speech develops, to avoid compensatory articulation / VPI; delaying past ~18 mo risks pathologic speech patterns
Alveolar bone graft (alveoloplasty) ~7–9 y/o (mixed dentition) Timed to permanent canine eruption — root ~1/3–2/3 formed, crown still partly covered by bone
Primary/definitive rhinoplasty ~14 y (F) / 16 y (M) After facial skeletal growth is largely complete
Verified: Standard sequence — lip & nasal repair in infancy, palate ~1 yr, alveolar graft at mixed dentition, definitive rhinoplasty at skeletal maturity (later in males). The palatoplasty-before-speech principle is why ~12 months is the target.

Cleft palate teratogens, bifid uvula, submucous cleft (+ OMC intro)

src/…616281.jpg

Verbatim

OMC Uncinate process ethmoid infundibulum hiatus semilunaris Bulla ethmoidalis Middle meatus Frontal recess --------------------------------- Cleft palate [?] of cleft palate Folic acid intake of mom Neonatal cheiloplasty Anti-epileptics → teratogenic → retinoic / tretinoin CAS – 18–20 wks Bifid uvula Submucous cleft 2 or more: ① zona pellucida – midline bluish line that lacks musculus uvulae ② notch at hard palate – "bony notch" ③ bifid uvula prone to AOM no attachment of tensor veli palatini

Interpretation

Two topics on one page: the osteomeatal complex (OMC) drainage anatomy and cleft-palate risk factors / submucous cleft.

OMC (final common drainage pathway)

Uncinate process, ethmoid infundibulum, hiatus semilunaris, ethmoid bulla, middle meatus, frontal recess. The anterior ethmoid, maxillary, and frontal sinuses all drain into the middle meatus via this complex — obstruction here is the pathophysiologic core of chronic rhinosinusitis.

Cleft-palate teratogens & risk factors
  • Low maternal folic acid — supplementation is protective.
  • Anti-epileptic drugs (e.g., phenytoin, valproate) are teratogenic.
  • Retinoids / isotretinoin (tretinoin) — potent teratogen.
  • "CAS 18–20 wks" = the window when a cleft is typically detectable on prenatal ultrasound (congenital anomaly scan).
Submucous cleft palate — classic triad (Calnan's triad)
  • Zona pellucida — bluish midline translucency where the musculus uvulae is deficient.
  • Notch in the posterior hard palate (bony notch).
  • Bifid uvula.
Verified & augmented: The tensor veli palatini opens the Eustachian tube; when the levator/tensor sling is abnormal (submucous or overt cleft), ET dysfunction → recurrent acute otitis media / OME. Bifid uvula alone is a common, often benign variant (~2% of people) but flags possible submucous cleft.

Interpretation

The named palatoplasty techniques — the high-yield comparison.

Technique Principle Note
Von Langenbeck Bipedicled mucoperiosteal flaps with lateral relaxing incisions; edges released and closed in midline Oldest; keeps anterior pedicles → blood supply, but limits push-back lengthening
Veau-Wardill-Kilner V-Y "push-back" — flaps pedicled on greater palatine vessels, retropositioned to lengthen the velum Lengthens soft palate; leaves a raw area anteriorly
Furlow Double-opposing Z-plasty of the soft palate Lengthens velum & realigns the levator sling; best speech & lowest OME rates; tension-free
Verified: These three are the standard teaching set. Von Langenbeck = bipedicle flap (1861); Veau-Wardill-Kilner = two-flap V-Y push-back (1931/37); Furlow = double-opposing Z-plasty (1986), which reconstructs the musculus uvulae / levator veli palatini sling and gives the best speech and hearing (OME) outcomes. ("Burdock" appears to be a mis-transcription; likely referring to alveolar/complete-cleft closure rather than a standard eponym.)

Musculus uvulae is highlighted first because its reconstruction is the whole point of Furlow — restoring the muscular sling that makes velopharyngeal closure work.

Sinus pneumatization (development)
  • Maxillary & ethmoid — present at birth.
  • Sphenoid — pneumatizes from ~3 yrs.

Nasal drainage & blood supply
  • Nasolacrimal duct drains into the inferior meatus via the valve of Hasner.
  • Kiesselbach's plexus (Little's area), anterior septum — anastomosis of anterior ethmoidal, sphenopalatine, greater palatine, and superior labial arteries; commonest site of anterior epistaxis.

Types of maxillectomy

Medial (medial maxillary wall ± turbinates), infrastructural (below orbital floor, includes hard palate), suprastructural (upper maxilla + orbital floor, spares palate), subtotal, and total maxillectomy. Interpretation

Diagnosing CRS (± nasal polyps)

Symptoms (nasal obstruction, mucopurulent discharge, facial pain/pressure, hyposmia) ≥12 weeks PLUS objective evidence: nasal endoscopy (polyps, edema, purulence) or CT (PNS without contrast = imaging gold standard).

Lund-Mackay CT staging

Each sinus system scored 0 (clear) / 1 (partial opacification) / 2 (complete); each OMC scored 0 or 2. Per side max = 12; both sides = max 24 — exactly her "one side 12 / 2 sides 24."

Verified: Lund-Mackay total range 0–24 confirmed. Her second scheme (0–3, polyp confined to / beyond the middle turbinate border) is the endoscopic polyp grade; the "Lund-Kennedy" score is the separate endoscopic appearance score (polyp, edema, discharge, scarring, crusting). She's conflating two named systems on the same page — worth separating for the exam.
CRS medical management
  • Intranasal / short-course oral corticosteroids (methylprednisolone/prednisone tapers) — shrink polyps, reduce inflammation; also used pre-op to improve the surgical field & reduce bleeding.
  • High-volume, low-pressure saline irrigation ("douching").
  • Long-term low-dose macrolide (clarithromycin up to ~12 weeks) as an anti-inflammatory immunomodulator (not primarily for infection).
  • Decongestants (short-term only).

Interpretation

Thyroid carcinomas
Type Key features
Papillary (most common, ~80%) Young females; psammoma bodies, "Orphan-Annie eye" nuclei, nuclear grooves; lymphatic spread; excellent prognosis. Assoc. Gardner's / FAP, prior radiation.
Follicular (2nd) Malignancy diagnosed by capsular / vascular invasion (not cytology alone); hematogenous spread (bone, lung).
Hürthle cell (oncocytic variant) Follicular-type behavior, oncocytic cells; can spread via lymphatics/vasculature.
Medullary From parafollicular C-cells → calcitonin; amyloid stroma; sporadic or familial (MEN 2A/2B, RET).
Anaplastic Elderly; undifferentiated giant/spindle "bizarre" cells; highly aggressive, poor prognosis.
Primary lymphoma Non-Hodgkin B-cell; strongly associated with Hashimoto's thyroiditis.
Le Fort fractures (all involve the pterygoid plates)
Type Pattern
I (Guérin) Horizontal — separates the alveolus/palate from the upper maxilla ("floating palate")
II (Pyramidal) Through nasofrontal suture, medial orbit, inferior orbital rim/floor, across maxilla and pterygoids ("floating maxilla")
III Craniofacial disjunction — separates entire midface from skull base; through zygomatic arch, lateral orbit, nasofrontal suture
Verified: The defining feature of any Le Fort fracture is pterygoid plate involvement. Le Fort II uniquely crosses the infraorbital rim; Le Fort III is complete craniofacial separation.
